Dear Door Openers: An Open Letter

As a 5 foot something girl with quite thin arms, I have always been aware of how weak they are. Be it the way I struggle to lift or carry heavy things, or how I get hurt if I wanted to hit or punch someone, as a joke. As one does, I try to adjust my life around it. I have to. Especially as someone who hates asking for help, and prefers to struggle to roll down a 25 kg (55 lb) suitcase on the bumpy pavement, wear her wrist and arm out than muster up the courage, and swallow the guilt that follows.

Among other things, one of the things I pay attention to, more often than I would prefer to, is: opening doors. An every day activity, I have to get into buildings, which happen to have doors. Simple, right? Apparently, not.

I usually have to lean back, put my body’s weight into it, so that I can use gravity’s saving grace to aid me. Or, if it’s a push situation, I lean forward, kick the door.

Problem solved? Mmmmaaybe?

Enter new variable: time. Are we surprised?

I can’t do it quickly enough, for other people are still waiting behind me. What I do is struggle instead, I cower under the pressure of not having opened the door for myself and the person behind me quick enough, only to realise that it is only goddamn too late and I’ve just caused a huge traffic jam of more than 2 people: HUUUUGE, mind you. Huuuuggeee in my standards.

So, a lot of context aside, it’s time to mention my tall people and/or friends. Thank you for existing. Y’all can see the door despite my existence in between, over my head, and still choose to hold that door, and do 90% of the effort that takes to open it fully. It surprises me every time, I marvel at my own (apparently new found) strength, and it turns into some pleasant gratitude instead. I owe you all for making my day, and there is nothing else that makes me happier than being helped by someone I can’t see, rather, can’t dare to make eye contact with, because I am too embarrassed by the inconvenience that I cause. I do mumble a thank you, but again, probably not loud enough for people to hear. Thank you for being kind, for being strong, and for choosing to open the door despite my attempts. Thank you for making my day, and my entrance into buildings with large doors easier than it is to do it all by myself. Thank you for carrying things that I could yet struggle to. I wish I could help, but I am either too embarrassed to, or I am not quick enough and I will waste your precious time.

And, to all the people I did not hold the door open for: I am truly sorry. It is not my intention to be rude, but it is beyond my arms’ capacity on some days to keep them open. I hope I can make it up to y’all on some other day, one that I can lean back, put my body weight into, and do it quickly enough. I’ll keep practising!

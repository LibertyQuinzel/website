Nail polish and peer pressure

Growing up a girl is a complex experience. One facet of this is the whole world of make up and aesthetics. To put in a couple of sentences: I was a tomboy with short hair, with minimal earrings, a lean figure who made button down shirts and jeans her whole fashion statement. There were never skirts, and then college happened. I swerved slowly and then all at once into dresses, skirts, earrings, the whole deal. But something that stayed with me since the shirt shenanigans: nail polish. Although it was banned in school (no bright nails, thank you very much!) I kept my love for coloured nails alive and intact, thankfully (Ammaji, thanks for the encouragement and for sharing your love for them with me). I have long nails, for the most part, and they stay painted. I have a good number of photographs of the nails, and even the slightest of chipping would cause me to re-do the whole set, in another colour, never the same one.

Painting my nails for aesthetics is the largest of the reasons. They look pretty, they make me feel pretty. There are videos on the internet about how having nails ensure you gesticulate a certain way, emphasising your hands, and thereby the coloured and long nails. I am one of those people, at least I thought I was. I felt I moved my hands a certain way, I placed things and held things a certain way (of course to cater to my unadmitted inability or disability created by said nails) to enhance the “experience” of having long nails.

The second reason would be a sense of control (are we surprised? hahah). Stripping down something that is well made, complete, finished, old and/or imperfect instills a sense of refresh in my brain. I take it off, and I see my bare nails and hands. They often look bent, a tad dirty, sometimes chipped/broken, and/or stained. I wonder if I would be able to go out with them looking like that. I wonder if someone would judge me as (or more) harshly as I am at that moment. I repaint them, giving them a new aesthetic.

I hate to admit it, but I believe I hide behind my pretty nails (I know the irony of hiding behind something that is the size of your finger nail is evident and funny). They make me seem put together, feminine, cool, and maybe beautiful (at least my hands are not so stubby). I do like feeling that way, but I also hate not feeling that way. I don’t like giving my finger nails so much thought, and thereby more power.

I let it slide, and I let myself hide behind my nail polished finger nails. To an extent. But days when I realize I am doing it for anyone else (dunno who that is) but myself, I chop them off. No long nails, no nail polish. Just me and my stubby, more functional fingers and their bare finger nails. They help me do so many things, they look bent, weathered, dirty and not so “polished” (haha pun) and I appreciate them for being there, taking hits, and being the reason for my abilities. The nails will grow pretty soon, and so will I, shedding the pressure I had created for myself. I look at them with a new perspective, whip out the bottles of bright coloured nail polishes and glitter, and know that I am doing it for one person only: myself.
